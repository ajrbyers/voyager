Rail

The right-hand metadata and status column on detail screens. The rail keeps status, workflow steps, and next-step actions visible alongside the main content - the reader scans the article on the left and the state of play on the right. See the peer review example for the rail in full context.

Rail sections

The rail is an <aside> of stacked .rail-section blocks, each with a heavily underlined <h2>. Typical sections are Status (an inset text block), Steps (a steps list), and Actions (an action menu).

Pairing with .layout-with-rail

The rail does not position itself - it relies on the .layout-with-rail utility, a two-column grid inside .app-shell-main (see Application shell). The main content takes the flexible left column; the rail takes a fixed right column sized by the --shell-rail-width token. Below 1100px the grid collapses and the rail drops beneath the content. If the rail carries the page's essential status and actions, add rail-first-narrow to it so it moves above the main content at that width and task priority is preserved; leave it off for rails holding secondary reference material.

When to use

  • Use the rail on detail screens - a single article, a review assignment, a production task - where status and actions should stay in view next to the content.
  • Don't use it on list or dashboard screens; those use full-width layouts and put actions on the items themselves.
  • Keep it to two or three sections. If the rail grows longer than the main content, it has stopped being a summary.

Notes

  • The rail is an <aside> with an aria-label describing its contents (e.g. "Status, steps and actions") so screen-reader users can identify and skip the complementary landmark.
  • Section headings are <h2> elements - slot them into the page's heading hierarchy rather than skipping levels.
  • In source order the rail follows the main content - so screen readers and the collapsed single-column layout both present the most important material first.
  • The primary call to action (e.g. "Make a decision") belongs in the Status section, above the steps and secondary actions.
